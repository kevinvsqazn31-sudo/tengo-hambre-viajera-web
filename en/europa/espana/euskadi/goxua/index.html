4 people

45 min

Allergens: GlutenEggMilk See details

Servings
4 people

Ingredients

Whipped cream

  • 500 gwhipping cream (at least 35 % fat)
  • 80 gicing sugar

Sponge and syrup

  • 12sponge fingers (ladyfingers)
  • 100 gsugar
  • 100 gwater
  • ½lemon juicehalf a lemon

Custard

  • 500 gmilk
  • 100 gsugar
  • 50 gcornflour (refined maize starch)
  • 3egg yolks
  • 1 tablespoonvanilla extractthe City Council recipe uses a vanilla pod
  • 1lemon peel
  • 1 stickcinnamon stick
  • butter, a little (optional)the City Council recipe adds it without giving an amount

To finish

  • sugar for caramelising, as neededor liquid caramel

Allergens

  • Gluten in sponge fingers
  • Egg in sponge fingers and egg yolks
  • Milk in whipping cream, milk and butter

Check the label of:

  • sponge fingers: they may contain other allergens depending on the brand.
  • cornflour: for coeliacs, it should say “gluten free”.
  • sugar for caramelising: if you use liquid caramel, check its label.

This list is based on the recipe's ingredients, following the 14 allergens in Regulation (EU) 1169/2011, not on specific brands. If you have an allergy or intolerance, always check the labels of what you buy.

Step by step

  1. 1

    Whip the very cold cream with the icing sugar and keep it in the fridge until the end.

  2. 2

    Make the syrup: heat the sugar with the water and the lemon juice over a medium heat for at least 5 minutes, until it thickens slightly. Take it off the heat.

  3. 3

    For the custard, bring the milk to the boil with the lemon peel and the cinnamon. Leave it to infuse for 10 minutes and strain it.

  4. 4

    In a bowl, beat the yolks with the sugar, the cornflour and the vanilla. Add a little of the hot milk while you keep beating, to temper them so they do not curdle.

  5. 5

    Pour the mixture into the milk and cook over a medium heat, stirring all the time, for about 15 minutes or until it thickens. If you are using butter, add it now. Leave the custard to cool, covered with cling film touching the surface.

  6. 6

    Soak the sponge fingers in the syrup.

  7. 7

    Build the goxua from the bottom up in a clay dish (or in small individual dishes): a layer of cream, then the sponge fingers and, finally, the custard.

  8. 8

    Cover it with cling film and leave it in the fridge for at least 2 hours.

  9. 9

    Just before serving, sprinkle sugar over the top and caramelise it with a blowtorch. If you do not have a blowtorch, use liquid caramel.

Tips

  • The cream has to be very cold to whip properly.
  • If you make it in glasses, you can see the three layers and it looks lovely on the table.

The dish

“Goxua” means “sweet” in Basque.

It is a classic dessert from Vitoria-Gasteiz, although today you will find it all over the three provinces of the Basque Country.

It has three layers: a base of whipped cream, a layer of sponge and a layer of custard, caramelised with a blowtorch.

How to order and serve it

Order it for dessert: it usually comes in a small individual clay dish. Push your spoon right to the bottom, because it is eaten from the bottom up and every spoonful should carry all three layers.

Quick questions

What is goxua?
The dessert from Vitoria-Gasteiz that you eat from the bottom up: whipped cream, sponge soaked in syrup and custard with caramelised sugar on top.
How long does the recipe take?
45 min plus 2 h resting. The recipe serves 4 people and you can change the servings in the recipe.
Which allergens does it contain?
Contains gluten (sponge fingers), egg (sponge fingers and egg yolks) and milk (whipping cream, milk and butter). Check the label of: sponge fingers (they may contain other allergens depending on the brand); cornflour (for coeliacs, it should say “gluten free”); sugar for caramelising (if you use liquid caramel, check its label). The list is based on the ingredients, not on specific brands.
Where is it from?
From Basque Country (Spain). Its postcard: Plaza de la Virgen Blanca, in Vitoria-Gasteiz.

About this place

It is the heart of Vitoria-Gasteiz, at the foot of the medieval quarter. It was left outside the walls after the city was extended in the 13th century.

In 1791 it was made smaller when the Plaza de España, the New Square, was built, and in 1917 the Monument to the Battle of Vitoria was unveiled in its centre.

Since 2012 it has had a plant sculpture that has become one of the symbols of the city.
